Player styles for Now Playing

One panel, twelve presentations. The SelectorBar switches between the flat cover and a player; the gear picks which player: a record on four kinds of deck, a cassette, open reels, a CD or MiniDisc, an iPod, Winamp, a hi-fi with VU meters, a Windows Media Player visualizer, or a drifting canvas. Everything under the artwork never moves. The tonearm is a real one: it cues, lifts, seeks, rides the run-out and returns to its rest exactly the way a turntable does.

Where the switch lives

Cover / Player is a two-item SelectorBar in the header row; the second item is named after the chosen player. The gear beside it opens Player style: three rows of thumbnails (Media, Devices, Software) and an Options block that only shows the rows relevant to the chosen player. It is a per-user preference, not per track.

Physically honest, drawn flat

Progress is always carried by the medium itself: the tonearm angle, the tape pack migrating from the left hub to the right, the laser sled walking outward on a CD, the click-wheel thumb, the Winamp position slider. Needle-drop ends with a 120 ms scale "thump" and a dust ring. A locked groove really is 1.8 s at 33⅓.

Every medium keeps the vocabulary

Pause stops the reels over 0.4 s, spins a CD down over 1.2 s, freezes the drift, lets the VU needles fall. Seek is a fast-wind on tape, a sled jump on disc, a wheel drag on the iPod. A new album ejects the cassette or crossfades the LCD.
