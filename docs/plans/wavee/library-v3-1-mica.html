Library V3.1 — one taxonomy, a title that never truncates, one trailing slot

The current V3 pane says Albums twice, two rows apart, and means two different things (a page, then a filter). It clips its own title to Your L… at 300 DIP, spends 30 DIP on a word strip that repeats the chips’ nouns as page links, and gives every row two trailing glyphs that eat the label. This is the redesign: the destinations fold into the filter they already duplicate, Liked Songs becomes the system-pinned row it is everywhere else, and the header follows a Priority+ ladder where the title is the last thing to go.

What is wrong today (from the screenshots)

  1. Two taxonomies, two rows apart. Liked Songs · Albums · Artists · Podca… (pages) sits above Playlists · Podcasts · Albums · Artists (filters). Same nouns, different verbs; nobody can tell which “Albums” they are clicking. → one set of nouns: the chips.
  2. The word strip is a second tab strip. It borrows the Home page’s underline-tab vocabulary for a different job (leaving the pane), shows four and a half of its five words at the default width, and its pager chevrons are hover-only. → deleted; the chips are the one band that scrolls, fade on at rest.
  3. “Your L…”. The inline search host and three icons out-rank the title, so the one word that names the pane is the first to be sacrificed. → the title is the last element to yield (Priority+), and it doubles as the collapse toggle.
  4. Home dominates. A 40-DIP Cozy row with a full-width selected plate, placed above the library’s own title, inverts the hierarchy. → Compact 32 with the standard 3-DIP gutter.
  5. Two trailing glyphs per row. Pin at rest plus “…” on hover cost ~56 DIP of label; “Can This Love Be Tra…”. The scrollbar thumb then overlaps the “…” column (image 1). → one 28-DIP slot; pin moves into the subtitle as Spotify does.
  6. Subtitle grammar drifts. “Artist” · “Album · Kim Min S…” · “50 songs” · “11 songs”. → always Kind · detail.
  7. The pinned band has no end. Only the presence of a pin glyph separates pins from the rest. → a hairline after the band (PinEndHeight already exists).
  8. Sort and View are buried. Folding them into “…” fixed the 7-icon header but hid the two controls a library list is operated with. → a quiet text control on the lens row, Spotify’s exact placement.

What the research settled

  • Filters, not destinations, are the sidebar’s nouns. Spotify’s Your Library sidebar has filter chips (Playlists · Artists · Albums · Podcasts), sub-filters (By you · By Spotify · Downloaded), sort (Recents · Recently Added · Alphabetical · Creator · Custom Order), library search, list/grid, and pinning — and Liked Songs is simply a pinned row. There is no separate “Albums page” strip; the filter is the view.
  • Where destinations are rows, they are plain rows. Apple Music on Windows lists Recently Added · Artists · Albums · Songs under a “Library” heading as ordinary sidebar rows, editable via “Edit Library Sidebar”. Nobody ships them as a word strip.
  • NavigationView says: pane title next to the toggle, ≤ 2 levels, labels always. Microsoft’s pane anatomy puts PaneTitle beside the menu button and recommends a shallow hierarchy; its top-nav guidance (≤ 5 categories, “icons cannot clearly describe your categories”) is what the strip leaned on — but top-nav is an alternative to a pane, not a band inside one.
  • Priority+ decides what yields. The pattern shows the most important items first and moves the least important into “more” as width shrinks. The pane’s most important item is its name; the least is the third icon.
  • Mode splits cost keystrokes. Hicks.design’s complaint about Apple Music — having to pre-select “Library” before a search finds “Cowboy Sadness” — is the same friction as choosing between two “Albums”. One noun, one meaning.
